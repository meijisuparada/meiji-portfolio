The Problem

A client email came in after hours on a Friday and sat unread until Monday morning. It only happened once — but once was enough to convince me it needed a permanent fix rather than hoping no one else got unlucky.

The Build

Built a monitoring agent on Gemini, wired through Zapier into Slack and Gmail. The hard part wasn't the AI — it was the layers around it: Zapier had no real documentation to learn from, the APIs were new to me, and the agent needed time-of-week awareness (weeknight vs. weekend urgency reads differently) plus routing logic to send each alert to the correct line-of-business channel instead of one firehose.

The Result

No more waiting until Monday. Urgent messages get classified and routed in real time, regardless of when they land.

The Question

Which CRM messaging, audience, and lifecycle strategies could lift conversion among existing customers while improving acquisition efficiency? Stakeholders assumed more personalized lifecycle messaging would help broadly — the tests showed the real driver was segment and offer strategy, not personalization for its own sake.

The Framework

Ran multiple A/B tests across email/CRM audiences over ~90 days: segment the audience, assign treatment/control, measure conversion and CPA, run the statistical comparison, turn it into a recommendation. I connected CRM results with paid-media and GA4 data so we were judging downstream conversion, not just email opens.

The Result

Roughly +5% conversion lift and −12% CPA over 90 days, which reshaped how the client allocated budget across segments.

The Finding

Mapped the consumer journey across awareness, digital research, and retail purchase. The opportunity wasn't a dramatic funnel drop-off — it was timing. Back-to-school consistently drove a spike in cleaning and home-care intent as households reset their routines, and the existing media plan didn't move with it.

The Shift

Recommended a calendar-based GTM approach instead of running the same messaging year-round: ramp awareness and consideration ahead of the seasonal peak, and lean the messaging into "getting the home ready" for the new routine.

The Result

Informed a full-funnel, calendar-based media and retail activation plan that fed directly into quarterly GTM strategy.

The Idea

An AI travel-planning assistant that combines natural-language trip planning with credit-card rewards optimization. Describe a trip — destination, dates, budget — and the agent searches flights and hotels while weighing your points, transfer partners, and redemption value against just paying cash. The insight: most people are sitting on points they don't know how to value.

The Research

No time for a formal study, so I ran rapid qualitative interviews with classmates and potential travelers on how they plan trips and use rewards today, and where the friction actually was — comparing options, and deciding whether to redeem or save points.

The Result

Scoped the MVP down to trip search plus rewards-aware recommendations instead of a full booking platform — and won 1st place, FinTech track, out of 80+ teams.

The Problem

Payroll tax isn't one calculation — it depends on employee type, location, pay frequency, and jurisdiction. The hard part was simplifying that without giving international students an answer that was technically misleading.

The Approach

Built and iterated the calculator with Claude, writing toward "what does this mean for me?" instead of raw tax terminology — explain the concept, then the practical implication. The feature I'm proudest of is an AI-guided explanation flow that breaks a question into its relevant factors, explains it in plain English, and knows when to say "this is complex enough that you should talk to a professional" instead of guessing.

The Result

Shipped and open-sourced on GitHub.
